## 1. ADHD MELTDOWNS & TANTRUMS ("ZERO TO 100 IN SECONDS")

### Avatar profile

- **Who:** Mom (late 20s to early 40s) of an ADHD son, usually 5 to 11. Some briefs use "mom of 2 ADHD boys".
- **The pain:** Explosive, hour-long meltdowns over small things: a "no", the wrong crayon, screen time ending, homework. He hits walls, throws remotes and toys, and screams "I hate you". The meltdowns happen several times a day or "5 in 2 days".
- **Her emotional state:** Exhausted and on edge. She feels like she's failing him, locks herself in the bathroom just to breathe, and is ashamed of yelling back. She's embarrassed when it happens in public (pickup line, birthday parties, Thanksgiving dinner).
- **What she's tried:** Telling him to "calm down", behaviour charts, magnesium, fish oil, Amazon gummies. Nothing touches it.
- **What she wants:** A calmer home, a kid who can hear "no" without exploding, and to feel like a good mom again.
- **Mechanism we pin it on:** "Cognitive dehydration" / the brain missing 7 essential nutrients. Meltdowns are a nutrition problem, not a discipline or mood problem.
- **Formats tested:** UGC mom tips (3 tips, tip #3 = nutrition), "30 days instead of magnesium", skeleton/doctor timeline, Pixar confession story ("I locked myself in the bathroom..."), and a doctor/pediatrician explainer.

### Avatar profile

- **Who:** Mom (and sometimes dad) of a kid aged 7 to 12, occasionally a teen, who is falling behind at school. Most winning ads frame the kid as ADHD. The non-ADHD versions frame it as a "nutrient gap" or "running on empty".
- **The pain:** Teacher emails and calls ("can we have a quick chat?"), "capable but does not apply himself", forgotten homework, 3-minute attention spans, nightly homework meltdowns, slipping grades and C's on the report card. The kid is fine at 8am and overwhelmed by pickup or 3pm. He gets the first half of the worksheet right and the second half wrong.
- **Her emotional state:** Frustrated, then guilty ("I screamed at my 8-year-old because she forgot her homework again", "I yelled at him in the school parking lot"). She's terrified he's being written off, or that he believes he's "the dumb one". She dreads the teacher conference.
- **What she's tried:** Tutor, screen and tablet limits, earlier bedtime, eggs at breakfast, a chewable or gummy vitamin, omega-3, magnesium, cutting sugar and dyes, reward charts.
- **The fork in the road:** The teacher or pediatrician suggests an assessment or medication, and she wants to rule out nutrition first.
- **What she wants:** The teacher asking "what changed?", "it's like having a different kid in my class", the first A in years, homework finished without a fight, and her kid feeling smart.
- **Mechanism we pin it on:** School drains the brain's tank (water, B vitamins, minerals) by the afternoon, which is "Cognitive Dehydration". CogniKid refills it every morning before school.
- **Formats tested:** Kid POV ("I have ADHD and can't focus on homework"), special-ed teacher's "3 biggest mistakes", Pixar confession, skeleton timeline, and the long-form report-card family drama.

**What the top performers do (Winning/Optimized ads below):**
- **Morning versus afternoon crash.** Nourify ("fine at 8am, overwhelmed by pickup"), VigorNatura ("focus all day, come home and crash"), Youth Sport ("hitting a wall at 3pm") and Saphire's teacher ("school drains his brain chemicals every day") all sell "school drains the tank".
